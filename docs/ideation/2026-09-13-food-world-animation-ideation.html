Ideation · animation review

Make every place move like it means something

Sichuan hotpot works because four motions tell one story: lanterns establish the air, leaves place the scene, steam establishes heat, and the broth visibly cooks. The rest of Food World should use the same density, but give each place its own verb.

Grounding Context

Live browser inspection confirmed the perceived gap. In Turkey and Xinjiang, clicking a stand surfaces speech immediately, but the food or stand reaction is often too small to read from the world camera. In the painted rooms, the safe reusable vocabulary is steam, fire, light, mist, petals, motes, birds, dust and rain; liquid requires a separately verified source and path.

Implementation correction · 2026-09-14
A rectangular crop is not a semantic animation mask. Several proposals below describe wakes, waves, pours or liquid ribbons that are only valid when the source painting visibly contains that exact material and its path can be isolated without touching a person, vessel or unrelated object. Never invent water, syrup, oil, food, utensils, shadows or handoffs on top of a finished painting. Prefer wind in an existing hanging object, a source-bound heat/light change, birds in genuine open sky or a short highlight entirely inside a real painted stream. This correction and the new-area methodology supersede any broader liquid recommendation in this ideation record.
Important working-tree safeguard
There is important uncommitted work. Before this review artifact was added, the branch was two commits ahead and had 13 tracked modifications plus six untracked paths. The changes form a substantial Turkey ambience/reaction and countryside pass, new generalized discovery cues, tests, documentation and accepted scene-image archives. Preserve them as work in progress; do not treat them as generated clutter. Existing photo-swap review/log files and image archives should be isolated from future animation commits unless deliberately included.

The most consequential code finding is structural: for every full painted room except hotpot, paintedScene() clears configured hanging props, foreground props and walkers. That explains why birds and hanging-object sway are configured in several China rooms but do not appear. Turkey's current work improves ambient layers, but the live scene still needs a food-specific focal action. In 3D, Xinjiang's shared hopFood and Turkey's small default serving-dish slide are too generic to communicate what each kitchen does.

Design standard
At rest, use at most three obvious continuous loops. On click, temporarily make one food/process action dominant. A nearby worker follows; at most one nearby person acknowledges. This keeps the scene richer than today without becoming more complex or noisy than hotpot.

One signature verb per place

Every room and stand gets one unmistakable action that names the place: pull noodles, puff nan, roll sarma, pour tea, drizzle syrup, winnow grain. Ambient effects support it, but do not compete with it.

Axis
Painted-room signature motion
Basis
direct: Hotpot's visible boil succeeds; other rooms often stop at steam, motes or light, while their captions already name a stronger process.
Rationale
The action explains the food before a label or story card does and makes regions feel distinct.
Downsides
Needs scene-specific anchors; a poorly placed overlay can look detached from the painting.

2

Source-bound material motion

Animate liquid in a finished painting only when the image already contains the visible stream. Use one or more short highlights wholly inside that stream, following its actual direction. If the source, destination or path is ambiguous—or the crop overlaps a face, body, boat or container—leave the liquid still and choose wind, light, heat, birds or another genuine layer instead.

Axis
Painted-room signature motion
Basis
direct: Generic curves placed inside water and vessel bounding boxes produced fake wakes and streams and crossed important subjects.
Rationale
A real painted stream can carry a readable glint; an invented one changes the scene rather than bringing it to life.
Downsides
Some attractive liquid ideas must be rejected when the exact path cannot be isolated in both crops.
Confidence
99%
Complexity
Low–Medium
3

Heat visibly changes the food

Do not use steam as the whole story. Let heat cause a transformation: bread puffs, a steamer lid breathes, a pot surface turns, skewers rotate and flare, coffee foam rises, and roast glaze catches the light.

Axis
Painted-room signature motion
Basis
direct: The engine already renders steam, fire and hotpot bubbles, so visible transformation is the next small step rather than a new animation system.
Rationale
Cause and effect reads more clearly than particles floating near food.
Downsides
Needs tight masking so the food does not appear to detach from the photograph.
Confidence
93%
Complexity

4

Three-beat 3D reaction

A click should create a short causal chain: the food acts first, the worker completes the action, then one nearby person acknowledges it. Speech can follow, but it should not be the first or strongest feedback.

1 · Food→2 · Worker→3 · One guest
Axis
3D food and stand reaction
Basis
direct: Live clicks showed immediate speech while food motion remained hard to see; current helpers move many foods and people with small generic amplitudes.
Rationale
The sequence makes the stand feel responsive without turning the whole crowd into a synchronized toy.
Downsides
More per-stand choreography than one shared bounce function.
Confidence
95%
Complexity

5

Environment as place identity

Select one environmental motion because it belongs there: canal ripples in Jiangnan, falling snow at a northern winter table, chaff at harvest, grape leaves in an oasis, a ferry wake in Istanbul. Birds become a rare accent, not wallpaper.

Axis
People and environmental follow-through
Basis
direct: The scene data already configures region-specific petals, mist, water, leaves, rain and birds; several China birds are currently suppressed by the full-painting guard.
Rationale
One grounded ambient cue adds life and geography without stealing focus from food.
Downsides
Some outdoor paintings have no clean empty area; effects need visual testing in both crops.
Confidence
91%
Complexity
Low–Medium
6

A readability contract across distance and orientation

Judge motion at the actual world camera and the phone crop. Reaction displacement should grow with camera distance, cycles should be staggered, and the clicked action should briefly quiet or visually subordinate background loops.

Axis
Visibility, pacing and accessibility
Basis
direct: Live inspection found reactions that exist in code but are not clearly perceptible in the whole-world view.
Rationale
The goal is visible motion, not merely implemented motion.
Downsides
Requires visual thresholds and phone checks, not only unit tests.
Confidence
94%
Complexity
Medium
7

A semantic motion vocabulary

Keep a small shared family of named motions—pour, knead, grill, puff, flow, harvest, serve—then tune subject, amplitude and timing per place. Reuse the grammar, not an identical animation.

Axis
3D food and stand reaction
Basis
direct: Turkey's in-progress helper already introduces semantic reaction modes, while Xinjiang still calls a broad shared hopFood.
Rationale
Future places become cheaper while remaining culturally and visually specific.
Downsides
A vocabulary can become generic if authors select a verb without grounding it in the depicted process.
Confidence
90%
Complexity
Medium

Xinjiang 3D stands

The current code contains many thoughtful individual movements, but the shared food hop and subtle body lean flatten their identity. Replace the generic layer with the following stand-specific sequences.

Heat and dough kitchens

  • Kebab grill: skewers turn first, coals flare, cook fans, one guest leans toward the grill.
  • Nan bakery: bread puffs at the tonur, paddle pulls it out, buyer reaches.
  • Polo kitchen: rice/carrot spiral follows paddle, cook stirs, one waiting bowl lifts.
  • Laghman shop: noodle rope stretches and slaps, puller's arms follow, one diner lifts chopsticks.
  • Oasis home: dough flattens under rolling pin, oven glows, child reaches toward finished bread.

Market and hospitality

  • Oasis bazaar: melon lands on scale, pan dips, seller fills a bag, buyer nods.
  • Grape courtyard: tea ribbon pours, grape cluster sways, host tilts pot, child raises cup.
  • Chaikhana: pot pours into bowl, steam blooms, server follows, one guest receives it.
  • Evening feast: platter turns/passes, host gestures, one guest reaches. Do not hop every dish.

Land, travel and landmark

  • Oasis fields: sluice gate rises gradually and the existing modeled channel responds.
  • Caravan stop: fire flares, camel responds, then the trader acknowledges.
  • Tianshan: the live painting uses mist and a bird while the waterfall silhouette stays still. The landmark is hit-only and has no separate modeled stand reaction.

Ingredient props

  • Fat-tailed sheep: step, head-turn and tail shake—replace the whole-animal hop.
  • Cumin stall: scoop cumin and release a bounded spice-dust arc toward the grill.
  • Yellow carrots: pull one carrot from the soil; tops shake and soil crumbs fall.
  • Apricots and walnuts: keep the current falling fruit, then land it in a basket while the picker bends.

Turkey 3D stands and props

Turkey's in-progress semantic reaction modes are a good foundation. The change is to make the specific preparation unmistakable and remove the default “serving dishes slide apart” response from shared tables.

Street food and ovens

  • Simit cart: one ring tips forward, seller hands it out, passer pauses.
  • Kebab: quarter-turn skewers, flare, cook fans, diner leans.
  • Fish: fish flips and lands, tiny grill spark, customer opens bread.
  • Pide: bread puffs, paddle advances, cook presents it.
  • Yufka: dough sheet billows and lands flat, roller follows.
  • Döner: keep continuous spit rotation; on click, a visible slice falls into bread. Rotating the whole meat faster is not enough.
  • Gözleme: keep the flip, but make it land on the griddle before the cook gestures.

Workshop foods

  • Baklava: syrup crosses the tray, pistachio sprinkles, baker tilts the ladle.
  • Coffeehouse: foam rises, cezve pours, guest lifts cup after the pour.
  • Dolma kitchen: leaf folds and rolls into place, helper moves the next leaf, pot lid breathes.

Shared tables

  • Tea garden: tea pours and one backgammon piece taps; one guest raises a glass.
  • Breakfast: honey ribbon lands on bread; host refills one tea glass.
  • Meze: oil drizzles onto one plate; server offers it and one hand reaches.
  • Supper: ladle turns the central dish, red butter/sauce swirls, one guest passes bread.

Harvest

  • Olive grove: keep fruit fall; land olives in a basket and let the picker shake one branch.
  • Tea hills: pluck three leaves and arc them into the basket; nearby picker turns.
  • Citrus orchard: keep fruit fall; use a heavier basket landing and picker reach.

Craft and water

  • Pottery: keep wheel and clay reshaping; increase silhouette change and add a wet-clay highlight.
  • Coppersmith: hammer must visibly contact the dish; add a small metal glint/ring and a shallow dish flex.
  • İznik fountain: pulse water, add basin splash rings, then settle.
  • Hammam: steam blooms from the hot room and condensation runs briefly; visitor takes one small step.

Landmarks and small characters

  • Mosque skyline: two pigeons lift and a window/minaret light warms; never bounce the building.
  • Ottoman timber house: shutter opens, flowerbox sways, resident appears briefly.
  • Bosphorus waterfront: broad ferry wake, one gull, boat horn represented by a tiny pulse—not more speech.
  • Cappadocia: balloon rises slightly and shadow drifts; rock forms stay fixed.
  • Street cat: tail flick, blink and stretch; no hop. Keep the quiet “Mrr…” response.
  • Tea sets and lamps: one lamp glow and a tiny glass-clink movement.
Small markers
For sumac/pepper, nuts, olives and tea ingredient markers, use a transfer into the nearest relevant dish—spice dust to grill, nuts to baklava, oil to plate, tea to glass—instead of an isolated bounce. The motion should show where the ingredient goes.

Rejection Summary

1One secondary environmental motion per roomKept only as part of the stronger “environment as place identity” direction.
2Identical four-layer recipe everywhereUseful ceiling, but an identical recipe would make regions feel templated.
3Birds in every outdoor sceneRepetitive; birds should appear only where scale and sky make them readable.
4Falling leaves in every roomBreaks place logic and makes leaves decorative wallpaper.

11Audio cue at every standPotential future layer, but not a substitute for the clearly visible motion requested.
