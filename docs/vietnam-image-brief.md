### The six China and Spain lessons

These rules appear again in every room prompt because they are acceptance requirements, not optional style advice.

- **(a) Moving cutouts hang from an empty support.** A moving sieve, herb bundle, banana leaf, fan or other hanging object is a separate pure-white sprite. The room shows only its empty peg, rail, hook or beam against a plain contrasting patch. Never cut motion out of a finished painting.
- **(b) Open surfaces and complete streams.** Every steaming pot is open. Every pour shows the lip, uninterrupted falling liquid and exact landing surface, all clearly visible and uncrossed.
- **(c) Portrait keeps every important subject inside the middle 80 per cent.** Signature action, discoveries, faces and empty sprite supports sit inside x `.094` to `.906`; only background occupies the outer strips.
- **(d) Plain contrasting backgrounds.** The patch behind each empty support and motion area is even, uncluttered and tonally different; no pattern, face, glare or deep shadow crosses it.
- **(e) Correct period, bodies, tools and food; no text.** No lettering, labels, signs, watermark or interface. Five-fingered hands grip correctly; food is the right scale and orientation; people, furniture, vessels and boats are physically supported.
- **(f) Two natural cues besides the signature.** Each room leaves one clean open fire, stove mouth or lamp and one clean opening, sky, water or doorway for code-added motion, with nothing painted through those patches.

## Task and reference authority

Create a production-quality illustrated asset set for Vietnam in Food World, an explorable place where food shows how people work, travel and eat. This is one compressed north-to-south journey across six neighbourhoods. Each room belongs to one cluster; never pile all of Vietnam into one picturesque collage.

The attached Food World paintings are the visual authority: painterly illustrated realism between cartoon and photograph; visible brushwork on timber, clay, cloth, food, smoke and water; coherent light; plausible bodies, buildings and furniture; slightly softened faces; rich natural colour; and three readable depth planes. Food and the working action dominate the foreground, people make the middle plane alive, and architecture locates the background.

Reject photorealism, photo compositing, glossy 3D rendering, flat vector art, thick outlines, chibi bodies, mascot faces, smeared hands, cinematic blur, collage, split panels, captions, filenames, watermarks, borders, interface markers, diamonds or arrows. The application adds its own markers.

Factual prompts cite sections and source keys in [`vietnam-research.md`](vietnam-research.md). Treat those citations as limits: do not invent an origin story, royal provenance or exact date the sources do not support.

Thailand is already covered. Do not recreate a floating market, noodle boat, curry mortar, Thai sweets kitchen, tropical orchard, temple compound, Andaman fishing kitchen, Malay roti room or Phuket tin-town room. Vietnam's boats connect places; they are not floating shops. [Research §9](vietnam-research.md)

## The period and the people

Recommended visual band: **Vietnam, 1900–1931**, subject to owner approval. It joins dated Hanoi vendor imagery, early phở, late Nguyễn Huế, historic Hội An shophouses and pre-1930s áo dài silhouettes. It does not prove that every regional dish below had its modern form in that exact band. [Research §8, V2, V3, V7, V9, V20](vietnam-research.md)

Use working clothes, not national-costume display. Formal or prosperous figures may wear loose front-buttoned `áo ngũ thân` with trousers. Vendors, cooks, porters and farmers wear practical tunics, loose trousers or wrapped layers in indigo, brown, cream and muted plant dyes; headcloths and conical work hats appear where useful. Do not use the tightly fitted two-panel modern áo dài, which the source dates to a 1930s redesign. [Research §7, V20](vietnam-research.md)

Vary age, height, build, face, occupation and gesture. Women cook, sell, buy, carry and eat in every cluster; children fetch, watch or help; elders work at a slower scale. Nobody poses for the viewer. Show four to eight readable people, plus only the background figures needed for life and depth.

No modern motorbikes, plastic, stainless steel, aluminium, electric light, gas, printed packaging, zips, logos, wristwatches or rubber sandals. The Saigon bread counter may include restrained late-colonial materials but no modern sandwich-shop fittings. The existing `motorbikes` id will be visually recast as shoulder poles, carts and bicycles if the owner approves. [Research §8](vietnam-research.md)

## Part A: concept image

File: `vn00_concept.png`. One wide image at the largest native size. It is a spatial and colour blueprint, not a room asset.

Show one continuous Food World table with six compact, separated neighbourhoods connected by roads and water: Hanoi guild street beside Hoàn Kiếm; Red River craft courtyards and wet fields; Huế wall, garden and Perfume River edge; Hội An rows fronting street and river quay; Saigon/Chợ Lớn shophouses; Mekong stilted homes, paddies and a separate Phú Quốc barrel shed. Keep the north denser and cooler, the south wetter and more open. Food rooms read before monuments.

Thailand is absent. No floating-market stalls, Thai longtails, karsts, Thai wat roofs, tuk-tuks, Thai curry mortars or Andaman beach. Vietnam boats are tied ferries and transport craft. Use period shoulder poles, carts and bicycles instead of motorbikes. [Research §2–3, §8–9](vietnam-research.md)

The concept must establish road access to all twelve rooms, visible cluster gaps, supported buildings, no houses embedded in water without posts, and no decor that reads as an unexplained block from above. No text or labels.
