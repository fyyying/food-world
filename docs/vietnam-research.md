# Vietnam: Stage A research

This is the research authority for a Vietnam build inside the `southeast-asia` world. It covers the existing `hanoi` and `mekong` area ids as one north-to-south package. It does not overwrite the completed Thailand brief: floating-market commerce, noodle boats, curry mortars, tropical orchards, Thai temple compounds and Andaman fishing kitchens remain Thailand's visual territory. [T1](thailand-research.md) [T2](thailand-image-brief.md)

## 1. Scope and source discipline

The recommended scope is six compact clusters and twelve rooms, split across the two existing Vietnamese area ids. Hanoi and the Red River belong to `hanoi`; Huế and Hội An also use `hanoi` as the code's northern-and-central routing bucket; Saigon/Chợ Lớn and the Mekong/Phú Quốc use `mekong`. The ids and the current Vietnamese objects come from the graph, not from a new geography invented for this brief. [C1](../src/fw/graph.ts)

This is a food journey, not a claim that one date, ethnicity or city represents all of Vietnam. UNESCO describes the lower Red River culture as a meeting of northern and southern influences, and describes Hội An as a trading port shaped by indigenous, Chinese, Japanese and later European influences. [V1](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1328) [V3](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/948)

Food claims below use official tourism, heritage, university, FAO and EU records. When a source establishes present-day association but not an origin date, this file says so. The image generator must not turn a modern association into an unsupported historical claim.

## 2. Landscape

### 2.1 Hanoi and the Red River

Hanoi sits in the lower Red River Valley. UNESCO describes Thăng Long as built on drained land reclaimed from the Red River Delta and as a political centre with nearly thirteen centuries of continuity. The Vietnam table should therefore read as a low delta city: water, flat cultivated land, narrow streets and dense roof lines rather than Thai limestone islands or an Andaman coast. [V1](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1328)

The Red River and Mekong deltas are Vietnam's two major rice-producing zones. FAO records manual transplanting as widespread in northern and central lowland rice, while direct seeding is common in the Mekong Delta. These are useful visual differences: hand-set green seedlings in the north; broader wet fields and canals in the south. [V10](https://www.fao.org/4/y4347e/y4347e1u.htm)

### 2.2 Central Vietnam

Huế lies in central Vietnam with access to the sea. The Perfume River passes the capital, imperial and inner-city complexes; hills and water are part of the site's planned setting. Use a river edge, masonry walls, tiled gates and garden water, but do not make the food room a palace fantasy. [V2](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/678)

Hội An stands near the mouth of the Thu Bồn River. UNESCO records a grid of streets parallel and perpendicular to the river, narrow rows of timber buildings, an open market and a ferry quay. That gives the cluster its physical grammar: river-facing shophouses and loading alleys, not a second Thai floating market. [V3](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/948)

### 2.3 Saigon, Chợ Lớn and the Mekong

The southern cluster uses a dense street-and-courtyard edge for Saigon/Chợ Lớn, then opens into the Mekong's channels and stilted domestic life. Vietnam Tourism describes ferries and sampans as everyday transport in the delta and records stilt houses, fishing and household work along the banks. Boats remain landscape support; they do not become another floating-market room. [V12](https://vietnam.travel/node/1011) [V13](https://www.vietnam.travel/things-to-do/colourful-charms-chau-doc)

Traditional rice varieties and rice-based systems have existed in the Mekong Delta for hundreds of years, according to a 2023 agriculture note indexed by FAO. The world may show a wet rice edge and household fish activity, but should avoid modern high-yield machinery and modern concrete embankments. [V11](https://www.fao.org/family-farming/detail/en/c/1632575/)

## 3. Architecture by cluster

### HN1 — Hanoi Old Quarter

Character: compressed guild-street frontage, shallow food shop, carrying pole, baskets and low stools. Vietnam Tourism calls the Old Quarter the historic heart of Hanoi's ancient guild streets and describes Vietnamese architecture, old shops and street food there. A Library of Congress photograph records the entrance to Hanoi's native business section; use it for massing and street density, not as a colour reference. [V14](https://vietnam.travel/node/915) [V8](https://loc.gov/pictures/resource/cph.3b32630/)

### HN2 — Red River craft village

Character: low timber-and-earth courtyard, tiled roof, bamboo screens, rice-steaming cloth and trays. The landscape is delta-flat and cultivated; the room is a working household, not a decorative pavilion. Northern lowland rice and manual transplanting are recorded by FAO. [V10](https://www.fao.org/4/y4347e/y4347e1u.htm)

### CT1 — Huế river and garden kitchens

Character: timber columns, masonry garden wall, clay tile, shaded veranda and glimpsed river vegetation. Huế was the Nguyễn capital from 1802 to 1945 and its surviving complex joins palaces, ritual sites, gardens and the Perfume River. The food rooms must remain ordinary working kitchens adjacent to that setting. [V2](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/678)

### CT2 — Hội An / Quảng Nam shophouses

Character: narrow timber frame, brick or timber walls, tiled roof, street entrance in front and service opening toward the river behind. UNESCO records 1,107 such buildings and the street-to-river loading pattern; it also records the town's indigenous and foreign cultural fusion. [V3](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/948)

### SG1 — Saigon / Chợ Lớn food street

Character: shaded street counter, deep shophouse, timber shutters, baskets and ceramic bowls; restrained colonial fabric may appear in the distance, never as spectacle. Vietnam Tourism identifies bánh mì and hủ tiếu as Saigon staples and describes street, wet-market and Chợ Lớn food culture. [V6](https://www.vietnam.travel/things-to-do/ho-chi-minh-city-cuisine-street-eats-fine-dining)

### MK1 — Mekong homestead and Phú Quốc barrel house

Character: timber floor above damp ground, open-sided cooking shelter, canal vegetation and a separate roofed barrel bay. Vietnam Tourism records stilt houses and household life beside small channels; the EU's PDO specification identifies Phú Quốc as the defined origin area for its fish sauce. [V12](https://vietnam.travel/node/1011) [V18](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/PDF/?uri=OJ%3AC%3A2012%3A001%3AFULL)

## 4. Food culture by region

### 4.1 Hanoi and northern food

Phở is a northern rice-noodle soup with a clear, long-simmered broth; official tourism sources name star anise, clove and cinnamon among common broth spices. A University of Michigan food-history profile places its emergence in northern Vietnam in the late nineteenth or early twentieth century while stressing its colonial context. The room may show a simple early bowl and shoulder-pole service, but it must not assert one disputed inventor or a single French derivation. [V4](https://vietnam.travel/node/195) [V9](https://rackham.umich.edu/discover-rackham/storyofpho/)

Bún chả is identified by Vietnam Tourism as a Hanoi Old Quarter speciality: rice vermicelli, grilled pork, herbs and greens, with fish-sauce-based broth. Its room is a charcoal-grill courtyard, not a noodle boat. [V4](https://www.vietnam.travel/things-to-do/21-must-try-vietnamese-dishes/)

Bánh cuốn is a northern breakfast made by steaming fermented rice batter on cloth, then rolling the thin sheet with minced pork, mushroom and shallot. Cốm is young green rice roasted, pounded and eaten in lotus leaf, with banana, in cakes or in sweet soup. This brief uses the two foods for distinct craft actions but assigns neither an unsupported origin year. [V21](https://vietnam.travel/node/1479) [V22](https://image.vietnam.travel/things-to-do/experience-poetry-autumn-hanoi)

### 4.2 Huế

Official tourism sources describe bún bò Huế with beef and pork-bone broth, vermicelli, lemongrass, shrimp paste, lime and herbs. The same source connects Huế cuisine to the former Nguyễn capital, but the room should show household and market work rather than claiming every local dish was court food. [V5](https://www.vietnam.travel/things-to-do/must-try-noodles-vietnam) [V2](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/678)

Bánh bèo is identified by Vietnam Tourism as a Huế snack. The second Huế room groups small steamed rice cakes and dumplings as a repetitive tray-and-steamer craft; it does not invent a court provenance. [V4](https://www.vietnam.travel/things-to-do/21-must-try-vietnamese-dishes/)

### 4.3 Hội An and Quảng Nam

Cao lầu is associated with Hội An by Vietnam Tourism, which describes thick noodles, pork, herbs and cracklings. Hội An's own heritage authority warns that proposed Chinese or name-based origin stories are debated; the brief therefore calls it a local Hội An dish without claiming a settled foreign origin. [V4](https://www.vietnam.travel/things-to-do/21-must-try-vietnamese-dishes/) [V15](https://hoianheritage.danang.gov.vn/vi/trao-doi-chuyen-nganh/chuyen-de-nghien-cuu-trao-doi/TAN-MAN-CAO-LAU-44.html)

Mì Quảng is identified by Vietnam Tourism as a Quảng Nam dish of broad rice noodles, a small amount of rich broth, fresh herbs and variable toppings such as pork, chicken, shrimp or fish. The room's signature is broth ladled sparingly, which differentiates it from Thailand's boat-noodle room. [V5](https://www.vietnam.travel/things-to-do/must-try-noodles-vietnam)

### 4.4 Saigon and Chợ Lớn

Vietnam Tourism identifies bánh mì as a Saigon-associated food, but the familiar filled sandwich is later than the proposed visual band in many historical accounts. For a 1900–1931 room, show bread, pâté and a bakery counter, not a fully modern sandwich claim; the owner may instead move the whole period later. [V6](https://www.vietnam.travel/things-to-do/ho-chi-minh-city-cuisine-street-eats-fine-dining)

Hủ tiếu is identified by Vietnam Tourism as a Saigon staple. The room may show rice noodles, a clear stock and a Chợ Lớn shophouse context, but the source does not establish a precise origin date, so the prompt must not do so either. [V6](https://www.vietnam.travel/things-to-do/ho-chi-minh-city-cuisine-street-eats-fine-dining)

### 4.5 Mekong and Phú Quốc

Vietnam Tourism describes bánh xèo as a Mekong Delta creation, with rice-flour and coconut-milk batter, turmeric, pork, bean sprouts and shrimp; it also explains the name through the audible sizzle. The signature is the batter spreading in a pan, which is not used in the Thailand rooms. [V4](https://www.vietnam.travel/things-to-do/21-must-try-vietnamese-dishes/)

The Mekong homestead room carries sour fish soup, clay-pot fish and rice as a family meal rather than another market. Fish and rice belong materially to delta life: FAO records rice-based systems and long-standing integrated aquatic activity in the region. [V11](https://www.fao.org/family-farming/detail/en/c/1632575/) [V17](https://www.fao.org/4/y1187e/y1187e22.htm)

Phú Quốc fish sauce is not a generic Southeast Asian fermentation claim. The EU registered `Phú Quốc` as a PDO in 2012 and defines the geographical area as Phú Quốc island and specified surrounding waters. Use a card-only barrel-house stop, not a thirteenth room. [V16](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/en/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A32012R0928) [V18](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/PDF/?uri=OJ%3AC%3A2012%3A001%3AFULL)

## 5. Ingredients and material cues

- **Rice:** show grain, rice noodles, wrappers, cakes and wet fields as different transformations of rice. The Red River and Mekong are both major rice zones, but their cultivation cues differ. [V10](https://www.fao.org/4/y4347e/y4347e1u.htm)
- **Herbs:** mint, coriander, basil and banana flower appear in official descriptions of bún chả, phở and mì Quảng. Keep herbs loose and specific; do not reuse Thailand's herb-basket composition. [V4](https://www.vietnam.travel/things-to-do/21-must-try-vietnamese-dishes/) [V5](https://www.vietnam.travel/things-to-do/must-try-noodles-vietnam)
- **Fish sauce:** use small table bowls throughout and reserve the large wooden barrels for the Phú Quốc card stop. [V16](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/en/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A32012R0928)
- **Star anise, cinnamon and clove:** these belong specifically to the phở stock display, not as a generic spice-market scene. [V4](https://vietnam.travel/node/195)
- **Lemongrass and shrimp paste:** these anchor bún bò Huế. [V5](https://www.vietnam.travel/things-to-do/must-try-noodles-vietnam)
- **River fish and shrimp:** these support Mekong cooking and rice-based aquatic systems. [V17](https://www.fao.org/4/y1187e/y1187e22.htm)
- **Lotus and tea:** use as landscape/card cues only unless a stronger food source is added before implementation; do not claim that either defines the national table.
- **Chicken:** keep the existing `chickenSea` card as a modest household ingredient and do not create a new chicken room; Thailand already has multiple chicken preparations. [C1](../src/fw/graph.ts) [T2](thailand-image-brief.md)

## 6. Everyday activity

The player should see food work before monuments: carrying poles arriving, charcoal tended, rice sheets lifted, noodles portioned, herbs picked, bowls washed, shutters opened, a ferry unloading and a family setting a low table. The 1931 Hanoi street-vendor photograph records shoulder-carried baskets and street selling; the Library of Congress record gives the dense business-street setting. [V7](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Marchandes_ambulantes_%C3%A0_Hano%C3%AF.jpg) [V8](https://loc.gov/pictures/resource/cph.3b32630/)

Water puppetry may appear as one small northern cultural landmark because UNESCO documentation calls it emblematic of Vietnam. It must not become the area's food story or a theme-park stage. [V19](https://ich.unesco.org/doc/src/01858-EN.pdf)

Mekong activity is domestic and transport-led: ferries, sampans, fishing, bank-side household work and food preparation. Thailand owns the dedicated floating-market performance, so Vietnam's boats remain connective scenery and do not carry duplicate vendor rooms. [V12](https://vietnam.travel/node/1011) [T2](thailand-image-brief.md)

## 7. Clothing and people

Use late Nguyễn-era working clothes, not modern fashion áo dài. Vietnam Tourism traces the modern áo dài to the earlier five-part `áo ngũ thân` and says the fitted two-panel redesign arrived in the 1930s. For a 1900–1931 band, use loose front-buttoned five-part gowns for formal or prosperous figures, plain tunics and trousers for workers, headcloths, conical hats where work requires shade, and regionally varied dark indigo, brown, cream and restrained silk colour. [V20](https://vietnam.travel/node/1216)

Do not dress every woman identically or turn working people into costume display. Huế may contain one more formal `áo ngũ thân` figure; northern vendors may use layered tunics and headcloths; southern workers use loose tunic-and-trouser forms. The source establishes the garment's evolution, not a universal uniform, so these differences remain a prompt direction rather than a claim of strict dress law. [V20](https://vietnam.travel/node/1216)

Hands, tools and physical labour must be credible: five fingers, correct grip, supported vessels, no floating baskets, and nobody posed merely to look picturesque. This is the repository's art-direction rule rather than a historical claim. [A1](art-direction.md)

## 8. Period band and why

**Recommended visual band: 1900–1931.** It contains early phở, late Nguyễn Huế, surviving Hội An shophouse life and documented Hanoi street-vendor imagery while staying before the fitted modern áo dài described as a 1930s redesign. [V9](https://rackham.umich.edu/discover-rackham/storyofpho/) [V2](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/678) [V3](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/948) [V7](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Marchandes_ambulantes_%C3%A0_Hano%C3%AF.jpg) [V20](https://vietnam.travel/node/1216)

This band is a visual anchor, not proof that every proposed dish had its present form by 1931. Official sources establish strong regional associations for the repertoire, but several do not provide origin dates. The owner must choose one of two honest routes before image generation: accept a visually coherent 1900–1931 world with carefully non-dated food associations, or move the visual band to the mid-twentieth century and commission new clothing/architecture research. [V4](https://www.vietnam.travel/things-to-do/21-must-try-vietnamese-dishes/) [V5](https://www.vietnam.travel/things-to-do/must-try-noodles-vietnam) [V6](https://www.vietnam.travel/things-to-do/ho-chi-minh-city-cuisine-street-eats-fine-dining)

The existing `motorbikes` object is anachronistic as a mass-street motif for this band. Stage A recommends preserving the id for compatibility but changing its future display to **street carriers**: shoulder poles, handcarts and bicycles. That recommendation is based on the 1931 vendor photograph and is an owner decision, not a Stage A code change. [C1](../src/fw/graph.ts) [V7](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Marchandes_ambulantes_%C3%A0_Hano%C3%AF.jpg)

## 9. Thailand boundary: do not recreate

Thailand already owns twelve room compositions and their corresponding signatures: floating market, noodle boat, palace household, curry mortar, sweets kitchen, Chinese shophouse, rice-field lunch, Isan grill, Lanna kitchen, Andaman fishing kitchen, Malay-Muslim kitchen and tin-town kitchen. Vietnam must not make renamed copies of those rooms. [T2](thailand-image-brief.md)

Vietnam instead owns these distinct actions: phở stock skimmed at a shoulder-pole kitchen; bún chả lifted from charcoal; rice sheet peeled from cloth; green rice pounded; lemongrass broth strained; rice cakes lifted from moulds; cao lầu noodles dressed; a measured ladle of mì Quảng broth; bread split beside pâté; hủ tiếu noodles blanched; bánh xèo batter swirled; and clay-pot fish glazed at a family hearth.

Shared Southeast Asian materials — rice, fish sauce, herbs, canals, baskets and tiled roofs — may appear, but the composition, food action and social setting must be Vietnamese and cluster-specific. This is the operational boundary between the two Stage A packages. [T1](thailand-research.md) [T2](thailand-image-brief.md)
